# Cover letter — draft for author review

Dear Editor-in-Chief,

We submit our perspective, “Dams, queues and droughts: reclaiming storage theory for deficit-accumulation drought science,” for consideration by *Water Resources Research*.

The paper addresses a specific limitation of anomaly-based drought indices: they do not directly represent how unmet demand accumulates under constrained supply, how that backlog is cleared, or how demand pressure can be distinguished from supply limitation. We reconnect drought science with the stochastic-storage and queueing lineage that hydrology itself developed, beginning with Langbein (1958), while explicitly acknowledging established work on soil water, groundwater, runoff, irrigation, water-supply reliability and high-flow queueing.

Our claim is deliberately narrow and is quoted exactly from the frozen 21 November 2026 statement:

> "Within Crossref, OpenAlex, the complete WoS S1–S5 / Extra A/B/C screens
> (142 records) and the completed Scopus S1–S5 / Extra A/C screens (295
> records), plus the citation traces, we found no study that puts all four of
> these together: (1) drought as an accumulated unmet-demand backlog with a
> finite-capacity state; (2) queue-state indicators reported as drought
> quantities; (3) non-stationary rates estimated from hydroclimatic
> covariates; (4) validation against exact reference solutions and
> pre-specified drought tests."

We do not claim that queueing in hydrology, stochastic storage theory, time-varying queues or machine learning for queues are new.

The perspective contributes an implementable mapping, a conservation ledger that separates stored water from unmet-demand backlog, and an identifiability-first research programme. A controlled synthetic example shows that identical mean arrival and service rates can yield a 2.3-fold larger peak backlog and a 17-fold larger critical-state probability when their timing differs. A 44-case sensitivity grid preserves the direction of this averaging effect. These numbers are synthetic and illustrative under the stated assumptions; they are not presented as empirical basin findings.

We pre-commit to held-out comparisons with climatology, SPI/SPEI/SSI and storage-based baselines, to uncertainty propagation, and to publishing a null or non-identifiability result if queue indicators do not add information. All numerical examples are reproducible from the accompanying code.

We believe this perspective will help hydrologists evaluate a mechanistic drought-state framework without overstating its prior art or its current empirical support.
