1.3 2. P01 — key objective

Plain objective: explain why drought can be read as a queue state, cite the existing water–queue lineage accurately, and set out tests that can show whether queue-derived indicators add drought information.

P01 is a review/perspective for Water Resources Research (NOVELTY_AND_PLAN_REPORT.md:55-70; START_HERE.md:21-28). Its objectives are:

  1. Reclaim the historical link between storage theory and queueing, beginning with Langbein 1958, rather than presenting that link as ours (paper1/P1_draft_v2.md:74-89).
  2. Map queue quantities to drought quantities: arrivals to demand, service to supply, backlog to unmet demand, capacity to a system limit, and reneging to abandoned demand (paper1/P1_draft_v2.md:103-150).
  3. Keep two dual representations separate: stored water as the buffer and unmet demand as the backlog (paper1/P1_draft_v2.md:123-150).
  4. Cite the established hydrology, irrigation, groundwater, runoff and reliability literature without overstating the gap (paper1/literature_audit.md:122-174; paper1/P1_draft_v2.md:152-187).
  5. Show why time-varying rates matter. As of 21 November 2026, the synthetic Box 1 gives 2.130 claims peak E[Q] versus 0.916 claims stationary, a 2.3× difference, and peak Pr(Q≥6) of 0.0733 versus 0.0043, a 17× difference (paper1/P1_draft_v2.md:266-292).
  6. State a falsifiable research programme with pre-specified metrics, baselines, null-result clause and guardrails (paper1/P1_draft_v2.md:294-347).

1.4 3. P01 — analysis protocol

This is a scoping-review protocol, not a full systematic review; the distinction is already recorded in the audit (paper1/literature_audit.md:12-18).

1.4.1 3.1 Search and screening

  1. Use the exact search strings and interface notes in paper1/scopus_wos_search_plan.md:10-61.
  2. Record for each search: database, interface, date, exact string, filters, raw hits, screened count, retained count and exclusion reasons (paper1/scopus_wos_search_plan.md:126-139).
  3. Screen the four pending WoS sets and write one keep/drop reason per record in paper1/literature_audit.md (paper1/scopus_wos_search_plan.md:70-74, :141-148).
  4. Add the required citation-trace seeds: Yang 2019, Harel & Mouche 2014, Mouche & Harel 2016 and Prajamwong 1997 (AGENTS.md:176-180; NOVELTY_AND_PLAN_REPORT.md:225-230).
  5. Full-text screen the retained set and keep the keep/drop table in the audit file (paper1/literature_audit.md:196-204).

1.4.2 3.2 Synthesis

  1. Build the concept map from queue term to hydrological meaning and caveat (paper1/P1_draft_v2.md:103-150).
  2. Keep prior-art rows with three columns: representation, what it does, what it leaves open (paper1/P1_draft_v2.md:159-175).
  3. Use only script-backed synthetic numbers. The anchor three-way check is exact E[Q] 0.91607 claims, RK4 E[Q] 0.91607 claims and DES time-average E[Q] 0.91295 claims, a 0.34% difference on 21 November 2026 (AGENTS.md:87-90; paper1/README.md:41-49).
  4. Label every pilot number “illustrative, under the stated assumptions” (AGENTS.md:47-52; START_HERE.md:87-92).
  5. Do not use raw CMIP6 output in any published number (AGENTS.md:53-55).

1.4.3 3.3 Language and gate check

Search the draft for strong claim words and “no study” phrases; check each against the scoped, dated gap sentence (AGENTS.md:30-45; paper1/P1_draft_v2.md:44, :349-362). P01 is stable only when Gate B is met (AGENTS.md:189-190).

1.5 4. P03 — key objective

Plain objective: estimate changing demand and supply rates from data, verify the estimates against exact mathematics, and test whether queue state adds drought information beyond standard baselines.

P03 is a methods paper for Hydrology and Earth System Sciences later in the roadmap (NOVELTY_AND_PLAN_REPORT.md:72-84; paper3/README.md:1-11). Its objectives are:

  1. Estimate λ(t) from demand covariates and μ(t) from flow and climate covariates (NOVELTY_AND_PLAN_REPORT.md:76-81).
  2. Validate against exact references: stationary-at-mean, Floquet periodic, MMPP/2 and discrete-event simulation (paper3/README.md:24-35, :65-88).
  3. Compare against constant rate, climatological rates, SPI/SPEI/SSI and low-flow statistics (paper3/README.md:113-130).
  4. Use held-out years, held-out basins and named drought events (NOVELTY_AND_PLAN_REPORT.md:258-263).
  5. Report a null result if queue indicators do not add information beyond baselines (AGENTS.md:59-61; NOVELTY_AND_PLAN_REPORT.md:260-263).
  6. Do not present machine learning for queues, time-varying arrivals or correlated arrivals as our contribution (AGENTS.md:62-66; paper1/literature_audit.md:144-174).

1.6 5. P03 — pre-registered analysis protocol

The metrics, baselines and null rule must be fixed before results are examined (NOVELTY_AND_PLAN_REPORT.md:258).

1.6.1 5.1 Work order

Order Work package Deliverable and stop rule
1 μ error near ρ≈1 Fix the dominant estimator weakness first (NOVELTY_AND_PLAN_REPORT.md:250). Current synthetic diagnostics show μ RMSE about 0.098/day and mass-calibrated overprediction under warming; treat both as diagnostics, not findings (paper3/README.md:81-84; paper3/src/evaluate_estimators.py:127-133). Add a test.
2 Floquet convergence Vary dt; report residual and peak-indicator stability. Keep the RK4 cap: generator reaches about 8.5/day and dt×
3 MMPP sweep Sweep switching rate and high/low contrast. The 1.16× amplification is one setting only (paper3/README.md:74-79; NOVELTY_AND_PLAN_REPORT.md:252).
4 Multi-gauge R pipeline Add gauge 4064 Yadgir while keeping linear BLOCK-1 scripts and base R (paper1/data_access_spec.md:47-55; NOVELTY_AND_PLAN_REPORT.md:253).
5 Zero-flow semantics Resolve gauge 4026 zero-flow meaning against the CAMELS-India documentation. There are 9,710 zero-flow days out of 14,308 non-missing days, 68%, and ρ must not be interpreted on those days until resolved (paper1/data_access_spec.md:59-61; AGENTS.md:163-166).
6 Claim convention Find real canal/crop records or run a documented sensitivity envelope; keep pilot numbers illustrative meanwhile (paper1/data_access_spec.md:19-39, :80-97). Change the convention in all required places in one step (AGENTS.md:73-76).
7 CMIP6 bias correction Bias-correct against IMD/CAMELS before use; raw CMIP6 and Hargreaves ET0 are pipeline checks only (AGENTS.md:53-55; paper1/data_access_spec.md:91-93).
8 Future μ(t) Use delta-change scaling of observed streamflow or a rainfall–runoff transfer function; CMIP6 extraction gives pr and temperature, not streamflow (paper3/README.md:144-146).
9 Validation design Pre-register held-out years, basins, events, metrics and baselines (NOVELTY_AND_PLAN_REPORT.md:258).
10 Uncertainty Propagate bootstrap intervals from rate estimates into E[Q], Pr(Q≥6), U and recovery time (NOVELTY_AND_PLAN_REPORT.md:259; paper3/README.md:30).
11 Ablation Test incremental information beyond SPI/SPEI/SSI and a climatological queue; record the null if none appears (AGENTS.md:59-61; NOVELTY_AND_PLAN_REPORT.md:260).
12 Writing Write only after Gate C (AGENTS.md:198-199).

1.6.2 5.2 Metrics and baselines

Use rate RMSE, peak E[Q] error, severe-state probability error, unmet-demand error, lead time and probabilistic scores such as Brier, AUC or CRPS where applicable (paper1/queueing_howto.md:170-180; paper3/README.md:117-130). Baselines are constant rate, climatological rate, SPI/SPEI/SSI, low-flow statistics and a climatological queue (paper3/README.md:119-123; NOVELTY_AND_PLAN_REPORT.md:260). Use block cross-validation, never random-row splits (paper1/queueing_howto.md:157-162).
